# Method: how each run is produced

1. **Candidate discovery (sightings).** JustWatch's public GraphQL `popularTitles` is queried for country US, objectType MOVIE, releaseYear 2025–2026 and monetizationType FLATRATE, filtered to each service's own package(s): Netflix `nfx`; Prime Video `amp` (included with Prime, never Amazon Channels); Hulu `hlu`; Disney+ `dnp`; HBO Max `mxx`; Apple TV `atp`; Paramount+ `ppp`/`ppe`; Peacock `pct`/`pcp`; Starz `stz`; MGM+ `epx`. The query paginates the full catalog, not just the top. A second source is Rotten Tomatoes' "New Fresh Movies on <service>" browse lists (cnapi, sorted newest, paginated back to early 2025 where RT allows; RT has no Starz or MGM+ filter, and its Prime list mixes in rentals). Any ≥80% title from those lists that was not already a candidate is looked up by title on JustWatch (`jw_supplement.py`). On 2026-10-24 none turned out to be FLATRATE-included with a 2025–26 first-release year.
2. **Identity.** Each candidate's IMDb id (from JustWatch) gives the IMDb title, year, type and top cast through IMDb's suggestion JSON. The RT page is found by RT search, matching on normalized title (JW or IMDb English title) and year (±1; an off-by-one year needs cast overlap). Manual matches are in `overrides/rt_manual.json`.
3. **Scores.** Every matched RT movie page is fetched and its `media-scorecard-json` parsed: Tomatometer (`criticsScore.score`, `reviewCount`), Popcornmeter (`audienceScore.score`, `bandedRatingCount`), and where-to-watch JSON. A missing Popcornmeter stays NULL and shows as "—" only after `audience_audit.py` confirms that RT really displays none: the movie page is re-fetched with a desktop UA, with and without the trailing slash, plus `/reviews/all-audience`, and the script checks `audienceScore.score`, `overlay.audienceAll/audienceVerified.score`, the `<rt-text slot="audience-score">` markup, the hero `collapsed-audience-score`, `reviewsData` JSON and JSON-LD. It also probes RT's `/napi/rtcf/v1/movies/<emsId>/reviews` API, which returns 401 without a login. The evidence quote is stored per movie.
3b. **IMDb rating (informational).** User rating /10 and vote count come from IMDb's official daily dataset `title.ratings.tsv.gz` (`imdb_ratings.py` → `imdb_rating_obs`, with the IMDb title URL, the dataset's Last-Modified time, and JustWatch's IMDb score as a cross-check). IMDb title pages answer scripted clients with HTTP 202 and an AWS WAF "Human Verification" page, so they are probed once per run (logged in sources_checked) but not scraped. Never a filter; ⁱ flags <1,000 votes.
4. **Eligibility.** Release year (rule above) is 2025 or 2026, the title is a film (not a short, special or series), Tomatometer ≥ 80 **and** a displayed Popcornmeter ≥ 80 (rule change 2026-10-24 r2).
5. **Availability.** A (movie, service) pair is *included* when JustWatch has a FLATRATE offer on the service's own package **and** the service's own public title page (the URL from that JustWatch offer) confirms it:
   - Netflix/Hulu/HBO Max/Disney+/Paramount+/Starz/MGM+: the title page loads (HTTP 200) and shows the title, with no "coming soon" / future availabilityStartTime.
   - Prime Video: the Amazon `entitlement-message` must reference Prime. watch.amazon.com bot walls fall back to primevideo.com/detail/<GTI>.
   - Apple TV: the page shows the subscription offer (free trial / monthly price), not just buy/rent.
   - Peacock: the /watch-online/ page says "You can watch … on Peacock".
   Reelgood (access_type 2 = subscription) and RT where-to-watch are recorded as extra cross-checks. Any disagreement is flagged.
   Rejections are kept in the DB: channel add-ons, rent/buy, free-with-ads, and bundle access.
6. **Ranking.** The main table (`v_ranked`/`v_top10`) contains eligible movies with ≥20 critic reviews, ordered by Tomatometer desc, then Popcornmeter desc, then critic review count desc, top 10 per service. Fewer than 10 are shown as-is. Side lists, which are never mixed into the top 10, are: `v_thin` (both ≥80 but <20 critic reviews), `v_no_audience` (Tomatometer ≥80, RT shows no Popcornmeter yet) and, collapsed in the HTML, `v_audience_below80` (Tomatometer ≥80 but Popcornmeter <80; these do not qualify).
7. **Flags.** † <20 critic reviews (side lists only); ⁱ IMDb rating from <1,000 votes; ‡ Popcornmeter from a 50–99 rating band; ʸ IMDb/RT/JustWatch years disagree; ◆ plan tier (Paramount+ Premium-only, Peacock Premium Plus-only); ⏳ JustWatch end date within 45 days; ⚠ availability note (Reelgood disagreement or mismatch, dual Disney+/Hulu listing).
8. **Labels.** genre_label and kid_friendly are hand-written from RT genres, descriptions and MPAA ratings (`overrides/movie_overrides.json`).

## Rule change 2026-10-24 (r2)
Requested by Emil after reviewing v1. The v1 ranking was Tomatometer first, then review count, so 100% titles with 10–18 reviews and no audience score crowded out mainstream hits like *The Sheep Detectives*, *Project Hail Mary* and *One Battle After Another*. Those titles were already in the DB and verified as Prime-included; nothing was missing from discovery.
- A movie qualifies for the main per-service table only if **Tomatometer ≥ 80 AND Popcornmeter ≥ 80**, both read from the RT page, **and** it has **≥ 20 critic reviews**.
- Ranking: Tomatometer desc → Popcornmeter desc → critic review count desc.
- Separate per-service lists, not ranked into the top 10: "Also qualifies, thin data" (both ≥80, <20 reviews) and "Critics 80%+, no audience score yet" (RT displays no Popcornmeter, confirmed by the audience audit with evidence). Movies with Tomatometer ≥80 but Popcornmeter <80 are listed collapsed as not qualifying.
- New IMDb column (rating /10 with vote count), informational only; ⁱ = fewer than 1,000 votes.
- The year, film-type, availability, Prime/Channels and Disney+/Hulu rules are unchanged.
- Views changed: `v_ranked`/`v_top10` now sit on the new `v_main`; added `v_thin`, `v_no_audience`, `v_audience_below80`, `v_latest_imdb` and table `imdb_rating_obs`; dropped `v_top10_min20`; `v_service_counts` gained n_thin / n_no_audience / n_audience_below80 / n_critics80.
